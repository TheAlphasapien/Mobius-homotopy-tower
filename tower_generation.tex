\title{Tower Generation from M\"{o}bius Holonomy:\\
Canonical Resolution of Representational Obstruction}

\author{Adam Kevin Morgan}

\date{\today \\ {\small Draft --- working paper}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We show that the M\"{o}bius connection $\omega = \tfrac{1}{2}d\theta$
on $S^1$ generates a canonical tower of algebraic and geometric
structures through iterated resolution of representational
obstruction. At each level, the subobject classifier of the
associated topos contains truth values that are locally definite
but globally unresolvable---the \emph{swap} values. These constitute
cohomological obstructions whose minimal resolution is unique and
forced, producing the next level of the tower. The first four
levels recover the normed division algebras
$\R, \C, \HH, \OO$ with their known algebraic properties
(commutativity loss at $\HH$, associativity loss at $\OO$).
Beyond $\OO$, the canonical resolution continues but outputs
constraint geometry rather than division algebras: zero-divisor
loci replace algebraic closure as the structural content.

The tower generation principle unifies several apparently
independent phenomena: the Abel--Ruffini obstruction to polynomial
solvability, the Conway Soldiers barrier at level~5, the
termination of the derivative tower's geometric content, and the
classification of finite simple groups (including the sporadic
groups). Each is identified as a projection of the same
dimensional obstruction hierarchy onto a specific mathematical
domain. The Monster group and Monstrous Moonshine are interpreted
as the maximally complex irreducible obstruction visible to
$\C$-level representation theory.

This paper is the fourth in a series. Paper~I~\cite{MorganI}
establishes the M\"{o}bius connection as the canonical generator
of arithmetic structure. Paper~II~\cite{MorganII} derives
consequences for the Riemann Hypothesis via orientation and
fixed-point loci. Paper~III~\cite{MorganIII} derives the
three-valued subobject classifier of $\ZSet$ and resolves
set-theoretic paradoxes. The present paper identifies the
mechanism by which the connection generates the full algebraic
tower and the obstructions that appear at each level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

The M\"{o}bius connection $\omega = \tfrac{1}{2}d\theta$ on $S^1$
is the unique flat connection whose holonomy group is $\Z_2$ and
whose associated double cover is connected
(Paper~I,~\cite{MorganI}). Papers~I--III established three
consequences of this object:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), nosep]
  \item The connection canonically generates $\C$ as a degree-2
  field extension of $\R$, with complex conjugation as the
  deck transformation of the double cover (Paper~I).
  \item The fixed-point loci of the identification structures
  on the canonical $\C$ determine the critical line
  $\mathrm{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ as the unique vanishing
  locus for equivariant sections, yielding the Riemann
  Hypothesis (Paper~II).
  \item The holonomy group $\Z_2$ generates the topos $\ZSet$,
  whose subobject classifier
  $\Omega = \{\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \swap\}$ provides
  a three-valued logic in which classical reasoning holds on
  the fixed-point locus and fails at the $\swap$ values
  (Paper~III).
\end{enumerate}

The present paper asks: \emph{what happens to the $\swap$ values?}

The $\swap$ truth value classifies propositions that are locally
definite but globally orientation-dependent: true on one sheet of
the double cover, false on the other, with no global resolution
possible within the $\Z_2$ framework. We argue that this
irresolvability is not a terminal condition but a
\emph{generative} one. The $\swap$ values constitute a
representational obstruction whose minimal resolution forces the
existence of the next algebraic level---and this forcing is
canonical: unique at each step, with the output determined
without remainder.

\begin{definition}[Tower generation principle]
\label{def:tower-principle}
Let $\mathcal{T}_n$ denote the topos associated to the $n$-th
level of the tower, and let $\Omega_n$ denote its subobject
classifier. The \emph{tower generation principle} asserts:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
  \item $\Omega_n$ contains truth values that are locally
  definite but globally unresolvable within $\mathcal{T}_n$
  (the \emph{swap locus} $S_n \subset \Omega_n$).
  \item The minimal algebraic or geometric extension that
  resolves $S_n$ is unique.
  \item This extension defines $\mathcal{T}_{n+1}$.
  \item $\mathcal{T}_{n+1}$ has its own swap locus
  $S_{n+1}$, which is strictly richer than $S_n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The paper proceeds as follows. Section~\ref{sec:swap-obstruction}
formalizes the swap values as cohomological obstructions.
Section~\ref{sec:level-one} treats the passage $\R \to \C$
(resolution of the basic $\Z_2$ swap).
Section~\ref{sec:level-two} treats $\C \to \HH$ (resolution
of non-commutative swap interference).
Section~\ref{sec:level-three} treats $\HH \to \OO$ (resolution
of associativity obstruction).
Section~\ref{sec:post-NDA} addresses the post-octonion regime
where the nature of the resolution changes.
Section~\ref{sec:convergences} identifies the convergent
phenomena (Abel--Ruffini, Conway Soldiers, sporadic groups)
as projections of the tower obstruction.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications.

\section{The Swap Value as Cohomological Obstruction}
\label{sec:swap-obstruction}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Review: the three-valued classifier}

We recall from Paper~II the subobject classifier of $\ZSet$.

\begin{proposition}[Paper~III, Proposition~3.5]
\label{prop:three-values-review}
The subobject classifier of $\ZSet$ is
$\Omega = (\{\mathtt{true}, \mathtt{false}, \swap\}, \sigma_\Omega)$,
where $\sigma_\Omega$ fixes all three values. For any $\Z_2$-set
$(X, \sigma)$ and equivariant subset $A \hookrightarrow X$, the
characteristic morphism $\chi_A \colon X \to \Omega$ assigns:
\begin{align*}
  \chi_A(x) &= \mathtt{true}  &&\text{if } x \in A \text{ and }
                                  \sigma(x) \in A, \\
  \chi_A(x) &= \mathtt{false} &&\text{if } x \notin A \text{ and }
                                  \sigma(x) \notin A, \\
  \chi_A(x) &= \swap           &&\text{if exactly one of } x,\,
                                  \sigma(x) \text{ belongs to } A.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

The values $\mathtt{true}$ and $\mathtt{false}$ are
\emph{transport-invariant}: they classify subobjects that are
globally well-defined regardless of sheet choice. The value
$\swap$ is \emph{transport-sensitive}: it classifies subobjects
whose membership depends on the orientation of the double cover.

\subsection{The obstruction interpretation}

We now give the swap value a cohomological interpretation.

\begin{proposition}[Swap as first cohomology class]
\label{prop:swap-cohomology}
Let $(X, \sigma)$ be a $\Z_2$-set and $A \hookrightarrow X$ an
equivariant subset. The set of elements $x \in X$ with
$\chi_A(x) = \swap$ is in natural bijection with the
$\Z_2$-equivariant $1$-cochains on the orbit space $X/\sigma$
that fail to be coboundaries.

More precisely, define the map $c_A \colon X \to \Z_2$ by
$c_A(x) = 1$ if $x \in A$, $c_A(x) = 0$ otherwise. Then
$\chi_A(x) = \swap$ if and only if $c_A(x) \neq c_A(\sigma(x))$,
which is equivalent to $\delta c_A \neq 0$ on the orbit
containing $x$, where $\delta$ is the $\Z_2$-coboundary operator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct verification. The coboundary $\delta c_A$ on the orbit
$\{x, \sigma(x)\}$ evaluates to
$c_A(\sigma(x)) - c_A(x) \pmod{2}$. This is nonzero if and only
if exactly one of $x, \sigma(x)$ belongs to $A$, which is the
definition of $\chi_A(x) = \swap$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The obstruction is non-trivial]
\label{rem:nontrivial-obstruction}
The key property is that the swap class \emph{cannot be removed
by any equivariant modification of $A$}. If $x$ and $\sigma(x)$
lie in different membership classes, no $\Z_2$-equivariant
redefinition of $A$ can make them agree without also changing the
classification of the entire orbit. The obstruction is
\emph{topological}: it reflects the non-orientability of the
underlying M\"{o}bius bundle, not a deficiency of the particular
subset chosen.
\end{remark}

\section{Level One: $\R \to \C$}
\label{sec:level-one}
%% ============================================================

At the zeroth level, the structure is the real line $\R$ with
its total order. The M\"{o}bius connection generates a $\Z_2$
holonomy that cannot be represented within $\R$: the involution
$x \mapsto -x$ (or more generally, any orientation-reversing map)
exists as an operation on $\R$ but cannot be \emph{absorbed} as
an algebraic degree of freedom within $\R$ itself.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical $\C$ generation --- Papers~I,~II]
\label{thm:C-generation}
The unique minimal field extension of $\R$ that absorbs the
$\Z_2$ holonomy as an internal algebraic operation (complex
conjugation) is $\C = \R[i]/(i^2 + 1)$. This extension is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
  \item degree~$2$ (minimal for non-trivial $\Z_2$ action),
  \item algebraically closed (every polynomial splits),
  \item the unique field with these properties up to isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The swap values of $\ZSet$ at this level have a specific
algebraic meaning: they correspond to the two roots of
irreducible quadratic polynomials over $\R$. The polynomial
$x^2 - x - 1 = 0$ (equivalently, $x^2 = x + 1$) has roots
$\varphi = \tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ and
$\hat{\varphi} = \tfrac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}$, which form a free
$\Z_2$-orbit under the Galois action. Complex conjugation
exchanges them. The golden ratio $\varphi$ is the
\emph{scalar residue} of this degree-2 swap: the irrational
number that $\R$ sees when it projects the $\C$-level relational
content onto a single real value.

\begin{remark}[Algebraic irrationals as projection residues]
\label{rem:irrationals}
More generally, for each prime $p$, the polynomial $x^p = x + 1$
is irreducible over $\Q$ (Selmer, 1956) and defines an algebraic
number field of degree~$p$. The real root of this polynomial is
the \emph{scalar residue} of the degree-$p$ relational content
projected onto $\R$. Its irrationality is not a property of the
number but of the projection: the relational content has
intrinsic dimensionality~$p$, and $\R$ cannot express it finitely.
For $p = 2$ this gives $\varphi$ (golden ratio); for $p = 3$,
the plastic ratio $\rho \approx 1.3247$; for $p \geq 5$, the
roots are not expressible in radicals (Abel--Ruffini), reflecting
the non-solvability of the Galois group.
\end{remark}


%% ============================================================
\section{Level Two: $\C \to \HH$}
\label{sec:level-two}
%% ============================================================

At the $\C$ level, the $\Z_2$ swap has been resolved: complex
conjugation is an internal operation, and the two-sheeted cover
is algebraically closed. However, new swap values appear when
\emph{multiple independent $\Z_2$ actions} are present.

\subsection{Non-commutative swap interference}

Consider a $\Z_2$-set with multiple free orbits. At the $\C$
level, each orbit's swap can be independently resolved by
choosing a sheet. But this assumes the sheet choices for
different orbits are \emph{compatible}---that they can be made
simultaneously without interference.

\begin{definition}[Swap axis]
\label{def:swap-axis}
Let $(X, \sigma)$ be a $\Z_2$-set with free orbits
$\mathcal{O}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{O}_k$. A \emph{swap axis
assignment} is a map $\alpha \colon \{1, \ldots, k\} \to S^2$
that associates to each free orbit a unit pure imaginary
quaternion (a point on $S^2 \subset \mathrm{Im}(\HH)$),
specifying \emph{which complex subplane within $\HH$} the swap
of that orbit is native to.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Non-commutative obstruction]
\label{prop:noncommutative-obstruction}
Two swap axes $\alpha_i, \alpha_j \in S^2$ can be
\emph{simultaneously resolved} (i.e., there exists a single
sheet choice that classically resolves both swaps) if and only if
$[\alpha_i, \alpha_j] = 0$ in $\HH$, where $[\cdot, \cdot]$
denotes the quaternionic commutator. When
$[\alpha_i, \alpha_j] \neq 0$, the two swaps are
\emph{irreducibly entangled}: resolving one constrains the other
in a way that depends on the order of resolution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each swap axis $\alpha \in S^2$ determines a complex subplane
$\C_\alpha \subset \HH$ (the plane spanned by $1$ and $\alpha$).
Resolving the swap along $\alpha$ amounts to choosing an
orientation of $\C_\alpha$. Two such choices are compatible if
and only if the corresponding complex subplanes commute under
quaternionic multiplication, which occurs if and only if
$\alpha_i = \pm \alpha_j$ (parallel or antiparallel axes).
For non-parallel axes, the commutator
$\alpha_i \alpha_j - \alpha_j \alpha_i \neq 0$ measures the
obstruction to simultaneous resolution.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $\C$-level collapse]
At the $\C$ level, all free orbits are implicitly assigned the
\emph{same} swap axis (conventionally, the imaginary unit $i$).
This forces all swaps into phase: they move in lockstep under
transport, and no interference is visible. The passage to $\HH$
is the recognition that independent free orbits can carry
\emph{independent} swap axes, and that the independence is
structured by the non-commutativity of $\HH$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Canonical forcing of $\HH$}

\begin{theorem}[$\HH$ as minimal non-commutative resolution]
\label{thm:H-forcing}
The quaternion algebra $\HH$ is the unique minimal extension of
$\C$ with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
  \item It contains $\C$ as a subalgebra.
  \item It admits a continuous family of $\Z_2$ involutions
  (one for each complex subplane), parametrized by $S^2$.
  \item The involutions compose non-commutatively, with the
  non-commutativity faithfully representing the interference
  between independent swap axes.
  \item It is a normed division algebra.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The algebraic cost of this resolution is the loss of
\emph{commutativity}: $\HH$ is non-commutative because swap-axis
interference is non-commutative. This is not a defect but the
\emph{faithful algebraic encoding of transport-order dependence},
which is the defining feature of the $\HH$-level obstruction.

\subsection{Connection to degree-3 structure}

The plastic ratio $\rho$ (the real root of $x^3 = x + 1$) is the
scalar residue of the first obstruction that requires $\HH$-level
structure to resolve natively. The Galois group of $x^3 - x - 1$
over $\Q$ is $S_3$, which is non-abelian but solvable: it
decomposes as $\Z_3 \rtimes \Z_2$. This decomposition corresponds
to a two-step resolution: first a $\Z_3$ rotation between complex
subplanes, then a $\Z_2$ sheet swap. The solvability of $S_3$
means this decomposition succeeds---the obstruction can be
resolved by composing prime-level steps, albeit non-commutatively.

\section{Level Three: $\HH \to \OO$}
\label{sec:level-three}
%% ============================================================

At the $\HH$ level, non-commutative swap interference has been
absorbed as internal algebraic structure. The new obstruction is
\emph{associativity failure}: compositions of three or more
transport operations can give different results depending on
grouping, not just ordering.

\begin{proposition}[Associativity obstruction]
\label{prop:associativity-obstruction}
Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in S^2$ be three swap axes in $\HH$.
The composition $(\alpha \beta) \gamma$ is always equal to
$\alpha (\beta \gamma)$ in $\HH$ (since $\HH$ is associative).
However, when the swap axes themselves carry internal structure
(i.e., when the $S^2$ parametrization is replaced by a richer
space reflecting the composition of $\HH$-level obstructions),
the grouping of compositions can matter. The minimal extension
that absorbs this obstruction is the octonion algebra $\OO$.
\end{proposition}

The octonions $\OO$ are the unique $8$-dimensional normed
division algebra. They are non-commutative and non-associative,
but they satisfy the weaker condition of \emph{alternativity}:
$(xx)y = x(xy)$ and $(yx)x = y(xx)$. The loss of associativity
is the algebraic cost of resolving the $\HH$-level grouping
obstruction, just as the loss of commutativity was the cost of
resolving the $\C$-level interference obstruction.

\begin{remark}[The normed division algebra sequence]
The four normed division algebras $\R, \C, \HH, \OO$ are the
\emph{only} finite-dimensional normed division algebras over
$\R$ (Hurwitz's theorem, 1898). This theorem is usually stated
as a classification result. In the present framework, it receives
a generative interpretation: the four NDAs are the four levels at
which the M\"{o}bius obstruction can be resolved by an algebraic
object (a division algebra). The fact that no further NDAs exist
is not a coincidence but a consequence of the obstruction
hierarchy: beyond $\OO$, the obstruction exceeds the capacity of
any algebraic resolution.
\end{remark}


%% ============================================================
\section{Beyond the Octonions: Constraint Geometry}
\label{sec:post-NDA}
%% ============================================================

The passage from $\OO$ to the sedenions is the most
consequential transition in the tower, because the \emph{nature
of the output} changes. Understanding this transition precisely
is essential both for the internal coherence of the tower
principle and for its physical applications.

\subsection{Why zero divisors appear}

The Cayley--Dickson construction formally continues beyond $\OO$,
producing the sedenions $\mathbb{S}$ ($16$-dimensional), the
trigintaduonions ($32$-dimensional), and so on. At the sedenion
level, zero divisors appear for the first time: non-zero elements
$a, b$ with $ab = 0$.

In the standard algebraic perspective, zero divisors are a
pathology---they prevent the algebra from being a division
algebra and are taken to indicate that the construction has
``broken.'' In the tower generation framework, they receive a
different interpretation: zero divisors are the \emph{algebraic
signature of internal cancellation structure}. Two non-trivial
elements composing to zero means the multiplication has become
rich enough to contain directions that annihilate each other.
The multiplication now has a \emph{kernel}, and that kernel has
non-trivial geometry.

\begin{definition}[Zero-divisor locus]
\label{def:zero-divisor-locus}
For a Cayley--Dickson algebra $\mathcal{A}_n$ of dimension $2^n$
with $n \geq 4$, the \emph{zero-divisor locus} is the set
\[
  Z_n = \{(a,b) \in \mathcal{A}_n \times \mathcal{A}_n \mid
  ab = 0,\; a \neq 0,\; b \neq 0\}.
\]
This is a non-trivial algebraic variety in
$\mathcal{A}_n \times \mathcal{A}_n$.
\end{definition}

The appearance of zero divisors at the sedenion level is
\emph{forced} by the tower principle. The obstruction at the
$\OO$ level is the failure of associativity---compositions of
transport operations depend on grouping. The minimal resolution
of this obstruction requires extending $\OO$ by a further
doubling. But Hurwitz's theorem guarantees that no
$16$-dimensional normed division algebra exists. Therefore the
Cayley--Dickson extension \emph{must} produce zero divisors:
the algebraic capacity for division-algebra resolution has been
exhausted, and the obstruction manifests as structural
cancellation rather than as a new algebraic operation.

\subsection{The geometry of constraint}

The zero-divisor locus $Z_n$ is not amorphous. It has a specific
geometric structure that encodes the post-NDA obstruction content.

\begin{definition}[Constraint geometry]
\label{def:constraint-geometry}
The \emph{constraint geometry} at level~$n$ ($n \geq 4$) is the
triple $(Z_n, \Aut(\mathcal{A}_n), \mathcal{E}_n)$, where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
  \item $Z_n$ is the zero-divisor locus
  (Definition~\ref{def:zero-divisor-locus}),
  \item $\Aut(\mathcal{A}_n)$ is the automorphism group of the
  Cayley--Dickson algebra, acting naturally on $Z_n$, and
  \item $\mathcal{E}_n$ is the \emph{cluster decomposition}:
  the partition of $Z_n$ into orbits under $\Aut(\mathcal{A}_n)$,
  each orbit constituting an irreducible ``zero-divisor cluster.''
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The cluster decomposition $\mathcal{E}_n$ is the analogue, at
the post-NDA level, of the irreducible representations at the
NDA levels. At the $\C$ level, the irreducible representations
of $\Z_2$ classify the swap content. At the $\HH$ level, the
irreducible representations of $\mathrm{Sp}(1) \times
\mathrm{Sp}(1)$ classify the non-commutative interference. At
the post-NDA levels, the orbits of $\Aut(\mathcal{A}_n)$ on
$Z_n$ classify the irreducible cancellation structures.

\subsection{Inheritance of lower-level structure}

A critical feature of the post-NDA regime is that the NDA content
is not erased but \emph{subordinated}. The constraint geometry
contains the lower-level algebraic structures as subobjects.

\begin{proposition}[Structural inheritance]
\label{prop:inheritance}
For $n \geq 4$, the Cayley--Dickson algebra $\mathcal{A}_n$
contains subalgebras isomorphic to $\OO$, $\HH$, $\C$, and
$\R$. The zero-divisor locus $Z_n$ is disjoint from these
subalgebras (since they are division algebras). The constraint
geometry therefore \emph{surrounds} the NDA content without
penetrating it: the division algebras are the ``zero-divisor-free
cores'' within the larger algebra, and the constraint geometry
occupies the complement.
\end{proposition}

This gives a precise sense in which the post-NDA levels
\emph{extend} rather than replace the NDA hierarchy. The
division algebras persist as distinguished substructures---local
regions where the full algebraic closure still holds. The
constraint geometry describes the \emph{transitions between}
these local regions: the structure of the space through which
one must pass when moving from one NDA subalgebra to another.

\subsection{The automorphism group transition}

At the NDA levels, the automorphism groups form a well-known
sequence:
\[
  \Aut(\R) = \{1\}, \quad
  \Aut(\C) = \Z_2, \quad
  \Aut(\HH) \cong \mathrm{SO}(3), \quad
  \Aut(\OO) \cong G_2.
\]
The exceptional Lie group $G_2$ is the automorphism group of
$\OO$; it is the smallest of the exceptional Lie groups and has
dimension~$14$. Its appearance at the octonion level is
significant: $G_2$ is the structure group that stabilizes the
octonionic multiplication table, determining which products of
imaginary octonions give which results.

At the sedenion level, $\Aut(\mathbb{S})$ is substantially
larger and its structure is less well understood. The key
observation for the tower principle is that $G_2$ embeds in
$\Aut(\mathbb{S})$ as the stabilizer of any octonionic
subalgebra. The \emph{additional} automorphisms at the sedenion
level are precisely those that rotate between different
octonionic subalgebras---the post-NDA analogue of the $S^2$ family
of complex subplanes within $\HH$.

\begin{remark}[Constraint geometry as ``orientation of
orientations'']
\label{rem:orientation-layers}
The pattern of the tower can be summarized as iterated
orientation layering:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), nosep]
  \item $\R \to \C$: elements acquire orientation
  (sign/phase).
  \item $\C \to \HH$: orientations acquire orientation
  (which complex subplane).
  \item $\HH \to \OO$: orientations-of-orientations acquire
  grouping structure (associator).
  \item $\OO \to \mathbb{S}$: grouping structures acquire
  cancellation relations (zero divisors).
\end{enumerate}
At each step, the structural content of the previous level
becomes the \emph{data} that the new level organizes.
Post-NDA, this organization takes the form of constraint
geometry rather than algebraic operations, but the pattern of
``meta-orientation'' continues without interruption.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Canonicity in the post-NDA regime}

\begin{theorem}[Persistence of canonicity]
\label{thm:canonicity}
The tower generation principle
(Definition~\ref{def:tower-principle}) continues to hold in the
post-NDA regime in the following modified form:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
  \item The subobject classifier of the topos associated to
  level~$n$ ($n \geq 4$) contains unresolvable values (the
  analogue of $\swap$) that encode the zero-divisor structure.
  \item The minimal geometric extension that resolves these
  values is unique: it is the constraint geometry of
  $\mathcal{A}_{n+1}$ (the next Cayley--Dickson algebra).
  \item The constraint geometry of $\mathcal{A}_{n+1}$ contains
  new unresolvable values, generating level $n+2$.
  \item At no level does the tower terminate or become
  ambiguous: each step is forced, though the nature of the
  forcing changes from algebraic closure (NDA regime) to
  geometric constraint (post-NDA regime).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof of this theorem requires making precise the
relationship between zero-divisor cluster structure and the
topos-theoretic swap values. The key step is showing that each
zero-divisor cluster at level~$n$ generates an unresolvable
truth value in the associated topos---a proposition that is
locally definite within any NDA subalgebra but whose global
status depends on which subalgebra one measures from.
This is structurally identical to the swap value at the $\Z_2$
level (locally definite but sheet-dependent), elevated to the
post-NDA context (locally definite within an NDA core but
subalgebra-dependent across the constraint geometry).

\subsection{Physical interpretation: confinement and
  the Standard Model}

The transition from NDA to constraint geometry has a direct
physical interpretation. At the NDA levels, the automorphism
groups yield the gauge groups of the Standard Model:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
\textbf{Level} & \textbf{Automorphism structure}
  & \textbf{Physical gauge group} \\
\hline
$\C$ & $\Z_2$ (conjugation) & --- \\
$\HH$ & $\mathrm{SO}(3) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)/\Z_2$
  & $\mathrm{SU}(2)_L$ (weak) \\
$\OO$ & $G_2 \supset \mathrm{SU}(3)$ & $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$
  (color) \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The $U(1)$ of electromagnetism arises as the dynamical marginal
mode at the $\C$ level (the phase degree of freedom that
survives projection). The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ of the weak
interaction arises from the partial projectability of the $\HH$
level: only one chirality survives consistent projection, giving
maximal parity violation. The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of the strong
interaction arises as a subgroup of $G_2$ at the $\OO$ level,
with confinement reflecting the non-projectability of the full
$G_2$ structure.

At the post-NDA level, the constraint geometry provides the
structural setting for the \emph{interactions between} these
gauge sectors. The zero-divisor locus mediates the transitions
between different NDA subalgebras, which correspond physically
to the transitions between the electromagnetic, weak, and strong
sectors. The Standard Model's specific coupling structure---which
gauge groups interact with which matter fields, and with what
strength---is determined by the geometry of these transitions,
not by independent parameters.

\begin{openquestion}[Quantitative predictions from constraint
  geometry]
\label{open:predictions}
Can the specific numerical parameters of the Standard Model
(coupling constants, mixing angles, mass ratios) be derived from
the constraint geometry of the sedenion zero-divisor locus and
the embedding of the NDA subalgebras within it? If so, this
would constitute the most direct empirical test of the tower
generation principle.
\end{openquestion}

\section{Convergent Phenomena}
\label{sec:convergences}
%% ============================================================

The tower generation principle predicts that several apparently
independent mathematical phenomena are projections of the same
obstruction hierarchy. We identify four such convergences.

\subsection{Abel--Ruffini and polynomial solvability}

A polynomial equation of degree~$n$ is solvable in radicals if
and only if its Galois group is solvable---i.e., decomposable
into a tower of cyclic (prime-order) quotients. Each radical
extraction corresponds to one step up the prime tower.

For degrees 2, 3, and 4, the Galois groups ($S_2$, $S_3$, $S_4$)
are solvable. For degree~5, $S_5$ contains the simple group $A_5$,
which is non-solvable. This is the \emph{same} obstruction as the
NDA boundary: the relational structure at degree~5 cannot be
decomposed into a sequence of prime-level steps, just as the
post-$\OO$ obstruction cannot be resolved by iterating the
Cayley--Dickson doubling.

\begin{conjecture}[Galois--tower correspondence]
\label{conj:galois-tower}
The Galois group of the polynomial $x^p - x - 1$ over $\Q$, for
prime $p$, encodes the symmetry of the representational
obstruction at the $p$-th level of the tower. For $p = 2$, this
is $\Z_2$ (resolved by $\C$). For $p = 3$, this is $S_3$
(resolved by $\HH$-level structure). For $p \geq 5$, the Galois
group is $S_p$ (non-solvable for $p \geq 5$), reflecting an
obstruction that no finite tower of prime-level extensions can
resolve.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Conway's Soldiers and the golden ratio barrier}

In Conway's Soldiers (a solitaire lattice game), a checker on
a half-plane can be advanced at most 4 rows beyond a boundary
line, regardless of the number of checkers used. The proof
assigns each cell a weight $\varphi^{-d}$ (where $\varphi$ is
the golden ratio and $d$ is the Manhattan distance to the
target) and shows that the total weight below the line is
bounded by $\varphi^{-5}/(\varphi - 1)$, which is less than
$\varphi^{-5}$.

In the tower framework, this barrier has a structural
interpretation. The game's jump rule is a degree-2 transport
operation (each jump involves two cells), and the golden ratio
$\varphi$ is the degree-2 prime irrational ($x^2 = x + 1$).
The barrier at level~5 is the point where the degree-2 transport
exhausts its representational capacity---the same threshold as
Abel--Ruffini.

\begin{conjecture}[Transport-degree barriers]
\label{conj:conway}
A lattice solitaire game with degree-$p$ transport rules (jumps
consuming $p-1$ checkers) would have its barrier determined by
the real root of $x^p = x + 1$ and would obstruct at a level
related to the first non-solvable Galois group exceeding
degree~$p$.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{The derivative tower}

The first derivative captures rate of change (a $1$-dimensional,
$\R$-native operation). The second derivative captures curvature
(a $2$-dimensional, $\C$-native operation). Higher derivatives
exist formally but lose independent geometric content: the
$n$-th derivative can be computed but cannot be given a native
geometric interpretation within $\C$-based analysis for $n > 2$.

The function $e^x$, which equals all its own derivatives, is the
unique fixed point of the entire differentiation tower---invariant
under all levels simultaneously. Its ubiquity in physics reflects
its role as the unique function compatible with the full
obstruction hierarchy.

\subsection{Sporadic simple groups and the Monster}

The classification of finite simple groups identifies every such
group as either cyclic ($\Z_p$), alternating ($A_n$, $n \geq 5$),
of Lie type, or one of 26 sporadic groups. This classification is
carried out within $\C$-based representation theory.

In the tower framework, the sporadic groups are the
\emph{irreducible obstructions visible to $\C$-level
representation theory}. They are ``prime'' in the group-theoretic
sense: simple (no normal subgroups), not belonging to any infinite
family, not decomposable by any systematic construction.

The Monster group $\mathbb{M}$, the largest sporadic simple group,
lives in $196{,}883$-dimensional complex representation space.
McKay's observation that the first nontrivial coefficient of the
$j$-invariant decomposes as $196{,}884 = 1 + 196{,}883$ is
interpreted as the \emph{fiber content / structure duality} at
the Monster's level: the identity representation (the base point,
the invariant content that projects cleanly) plus the first
irreducible fiber (the smallest non-trivial representation of the
obstruction).

\begin{conjecture}[Monster as representational wall]
\label{conj:monster}
The Monster group is the maximally complex irreducible obstruction
expressible within $\C$-level representation theory. The Griess
algebra (the $196{,}884$-dimensional commutative non-associative
algebra in which $\mathbb{M}$ was constructed) sits at the
algebraic threshold between $\C$ (commutative, associative) and
$\OO$ (non-commutative, non-associative), and its non-associativity
is the algebraic signature of post-$\C$ obstruction content
compressed into $\C$-level representation.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The Langlands Program as Tower Cartography}
\label{sec:langlands}
%% ============================================================

The Langlands program studies the correspondence between Galois
representations (how roots of polynomials transform under
symmetry) and automorphic forms (analytic objects on specific
groups). In the tower framework, this is the systematic study of
how irreducible relational content at each prime level projects
into the representation schemes available at each level of the
tower.

The $j$-function, central to Monstrous Moonshine, is an
automorphic form for $\mathrm{SL}(2, \Z)$ acting on the upper
half of $\C$. Its Fourier coefficients encode the irreducible
representations of the Monster. The Langlands program generalizes
this to all reductive groups and all number fields. In the tower
framework, this generalization corresponds to mapping the full
prime hierarchy---not just the degree-2 structure visible through
the $j$-function, but the higher-degree obstructions at every
prime level---into the analytic objects available at each
representational level.

\begin{openquestion}[Langlands and the quaternionic level]
\label{open:langlands-H}
Does a quaternionic analog of the Langlands correspondence
exist---a systematic relationship between non-abelian Galois
representations (the $\HH$-level obstructions) and automorphic
forms on groups that natively represent non-commutative transport?
If so, it would constitute the ``$\HH$-level cartography'' of
the tower, resolving structures that the $\C$-level Langlands
program can only encode indirectly.
\end{openquestion}

\subsection{Summary of the tower}

\end{tabular}

\subsection{The static/dynamic duality}

The M\"{o}bius structure, considered statically, is the complete
tower---all levels present simultaneously as aspects of one
object. Considered dynamically, it is the traversal: the
holonomy being accumulated, the ambiguity being encountered and
resolved, the levels appearing sequentially as the connection is
followed. These are not two different objects. The static
structure \emph{is} the completed dynamic process, and the
dynamic process \emph{is} the static structure being traversed.

This duality is reflected in the relationship between the
division algebra tower (the static hierarchy of algebraic
objects) and the tower generation principle (the dynamic process
of obstruction and resolution). The tower does not exist prior
to its generation, nor is it generated and then frozen. It is
the canonical form of iterating ambiguity resolution, which is
simultaneously a completed object and an ongoing process.

\subsection{Relationship to physics}

The tower generation principle has physical consequences that are
explored in detail in the companion papers. The gauge groups of
the Standard Model---$U(1)$, $SU(2)$, $SU(3)$---emerge at
specific levels of the tower as the automorphism groups of the
representational capacity at each level, not as assumed symmetries.
The loss of commutativity at the $\HH$ level corresponds to the
chiral structure of the weak interaction; the loss of
associativity at the $\OO$ level corresponds to the confinement
of the strong interaction. These correspondences, and their
quantitative consequences, will be developed in subsequent work.
